What is a face-down study timer, and when should you use one?

Short answer: A face-down study timer records study time only while your phone lies screen-down, and pauses when you pick the phone up. Use one if your phone sits on your desk while you study and you keep reaching for it; the total it shows is the time you actually left the phone alone. If you need apps blocked, breaks scheduled for you, or you study on the phone itself, another tool fits better.

When Studydown fits

Studydown is a face-down timer for iPhone: choose an activity, tap start, then place the phone face down on a steady surface and keep Studydown open. It's a good fit if:

  • Your phone sits on the desk while you study, and the problem is picking it up, not one particular app.
  • You want an accurate total of study hours per subject or project, for example while preparing for an exam or a certification.
  • You have a deadline and a target number of hours, and want to see the daily pace you still need (goals and deadlines are part of the one-time Pro purchase).
  • You want to know how often you picked up your phone, not only how long you studied.
  • You would rather not create an account: timing and records work on your device, with no account and no ads.

When something else fits better

  • You need apps blocked. Studydown does not block apps. Use Screen Time app limits on iPhone (which you can override on your own device) or an app blocker.
  • You study on the phone itself. Flashcards, lecture videos or an e-textbook on the phone will not count, because picking it up pauses the count and leaving the app stops it. Use a stopwatch, or add the time to Studydown manually afterwards.
  • You want the timer to schedule breaks. Use a Pomodoro timer. Studydown's listing does not describe a work-and-break cycle.
  • You only want fewer interruptions. A Focus on iPhone silences notifications except from the people and apps you allow. It is built in and does not record study time.
  • You want no phone on the desk at all. A kitchen timer and a paper log let you leave the phone in another room. You total the hours yourself.
  • You need a single countdown, such as a timed practice paper. The built-in Clock timer does that. It keeps running whether or not you pick up the phone.

Questions

Does Studydown block other apps?

No. Studydown does not lock or limit other apps. It records time while your phone is face down and Studydown is open; leaving the app stops the count. If you want apps blocked, use Screen Time limits or an app blocker.

Can I study on my phone while Studydown is timing?

Not in a way it will count. Picking up the phone pauses the count after your chosen grace period, and leaving the app stops it. If you study with flashcards or videos on the phone, you can add that time manually afterwards.

Is a face-down timer the same as a Pomodoro timer?

No. A Pomodoro timer splits work into fixed intervals with breaks between them. A face-down timer measures how long the phone stayed down. Studydown's App Store listing does not describe a work-and-break cycle.

Does Studydown silence notifications?

Its App Store listing does not describe silencing notifications. If you want that as well, turn on a Focus on your iPhone before you start.

When is a paper log or a kitchen timer the better choice?

When you want no phone on the desk at all, for example in another room during a mock exam. You give up the automatic per-subject totals, pickup count and deadline pace, and add up the hours yourself.
